{{/* Hero — left portrait + intro, right pink "view portfolio" aside. Why this needed another pass: the previous version scaled cleanly at the couple of sizes it was checked against (a phone, a 2K+ monitor) but was unreliable everywhere in between — the exact range that covers most laptops and every 1080p monitor. Two root causes: 1. The portrait's width was `md:w-[calc(30%_+_30px)]` — 30% of its *flex container's* width, which itself depends on how much room is left after the aside, which itself was a fraction of the *outer section's* width. Three nested percentages meant the portrait's actual rendered size drifted around unpredictably across the laptop/1080p range depending on exactly how those percentages composed at that particular width — it wasn't a bug exactly, just impossible to reason about or tune. 2. The right-hand aside was a flat `md:w-1/5` of the *entire section*, with no ceiling. On a 1080p+ monitor that's 380px+ just for a "view portfolio" button; on 2K/4K it becomes genuinely huge and visually outweighs the (separately width-capped) portrait+text column next to it — the two sides stop looking like they belong to the same layout. The fix: replace both the portrait width and the aside width with `clamp()` — a minimum, a viewport-relative preferred size, and a maximum. That scales continuously with the actual viewport width (laptop, 1080p, 2K, 4K — every value in between, not just the breakpoints we happened to test) and, critically, has a hard ceiling so nothing balloons on a big monitor. The main headline and the pull quote get the same treatment for the same reason — the old `text-3xl sm:text-4xl` jump either looked slightly small on a 1440px laptop or slightly large on a 1920px monitor depending on which side of the jump you landed on; a fluid clamp lands right for both. */ -}}
